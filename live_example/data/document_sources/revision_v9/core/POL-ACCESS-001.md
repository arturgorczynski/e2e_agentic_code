# Identity and access to company information

## Acting identity

The portal selects an acting customer or employee profile for this fictional
company demonstration. The application supplies the selected profile and its
permissions to tools. A name or ID typed into a chat message does not change
that acting identity. Profile switching is visible in the interface and applies
to subsequent requests; private work remains attached to its original owner.

## Customer and employee scope

A customer may read records and documents assigned to that customer scope.
Employee scopes expose the business functions assigned to the selected profile:
commercial, finance, operations, support or analytics. The document library
applies access rules before returning search snippets. The employee directory
contains business assignments, not compensation or banking details. Super-user
controls in this demonstration provide broad company visibility but do not
erase authorship or change the owner of a private conversation or report.

## Private work and operations

Conversations, pending decisions, traces, saved reports and exports carry an
owner. Check that owner when listing, reading, resuming and downloading them.
An approval authorizes the specific proposed action within the actor's existing
scope; it does not grant new finance or operations rights. Recheck scope and
source versions after an interrupted operation. A missing record remains
missing; do not substitute another customer's order or infer a status.

## Demonstration boundary

The portal uses fictional profiles and data for a local demonstration. Profile
selection is a test control, not production authentication. No real customer
identity, email delivery, payment collection or external system integration is
implied. Keep source-history records from 2011 distinct from contemporary
fictional history when presenting a timeline or report.
